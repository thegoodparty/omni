# Example Roadmap Package (fabricated)

A worked example of a finished roadmap package, so you can see what the three deliverables look like before running your own. **Everything here is invented.** Jane Doe, Riverton, the outlets, the vote counts, and the colleagues are all fictional placeholders for illustration. Do not treat any figure as real.

Companion to `books/generate-governing-roadmap.md` (the procedure) and `books/run-your-first-roadmap.md` (the quickstart).

The example EO: **Jane Doe, At-Large Member, Riverton City Council.** Won a contested 2025 at-large race. Seven-member council plus an elected mayor; four votes carry a measure. Next election November 2027.

---

## 1. D-Short (tactical action plan) — abbreviated

> **Your situation.** You won your at-large seat with 4,235 votes on a platform of school funding, transparency, and housing. On the current council you are part of a three-member group that has lost several 3-4 votes this year. Your task for the next six months is to turn one or two colleagues into reliable partners on the issues you ran on.

> **Your moves.**
> - **Move A — The FY27 budget vote (before May 15).** The school line is the vote that defines your first year. Confirm the level-services number with the finance staff, signal your yes publicly if schools are held harmless, and post a short explanation to constituents the day after.
> - **Move B — Build from three to four.** Councillor Smith is the most reachable partner on schools; Councillor Lee is reachable on procedure. Have a one-on-one with each before the budget vote.
> - **Move C — Make transparency concrete.** Move your transparency promise from rhetoric to a standing practice: a monthly written update and a request that the relevant committee hold one open hearing this fall.
> - **Move D — Show up where voters already are.** Keep the neighborhood forum you started during the campaign. It is your distinctive asset.

> **Council map.** Seven members; you need four. Smith (likely partner on schools), Lee (procedure), Garcia (swing), three current opponents. Mayor breaks ties on procedural matters only.

> **Calendar.** Apr-May: budget. Summer: transparency practice live. Fall: committee hearing. 2027: reelection filing window (confirm exact date).

---

## 2. D-Long (full roadmap) — section outline

The D-Long is ~20-25 pages. It is not reproduced in full here; this is the section skeleton, with a sample of two sections so you can see the depth and citation style.

1. **Situation** — where the EO stands, the mandate, and the central tension.
2. **Government structure** — body size, terms, thresholds, the executive's role.
3. **Vote map** — recent contested votes and who voted how.
4. **Priorities with political math** — the issues, ranked, with what it takes to win each.
5. **Constituency model** — modeled voter priorities (0-100), clearly labeled as modeled.
6. **Phased calendar** — dated actions across the term.
7. **Risk register** — what could go wrong, probability, impact, mitigation.
8. **Reelection narrative** — the story the EO is building toward 2027.
9. **GoodParty support** — what we help with and how to reach us.
10. **Methodology** — sources, source tiers, and how modeled data was derived.

> **Sample, Section 1 (Situation).** You enter the council with a clear at-large mandate (4,235 votes, second-highest of four candidates, per the Riverton Gazette, Nov 5 2025) but without the votes to carry it alone. The recurring 3-4 losses this spring are a procedural problem, not an ideological one: on schools and transparency your positions track a majority of residents, but you have not yet converted that alignment into a fourth vote on the dais.

> **Sample, Section 5 (Constituency model, modeled).** Modeled resident priorities, on a 0-100 salience scale derived from 2025 election themes, candidate forums, and jurisdiction-level Census data (modeled, not measured; to be replaced when polling runs): school funding 86, public safety 71, housing affordability 64, transparency 61, roads 52. Treat these as directional, not precise.

## 3. AM summary (internal — what you read before sending)

This is the artifact you act on. It is never sent to the EO.

> **Scoring snapshot.** D-Long: SEND (combined 7.8). D-Short: SEND (combined 7.2). Both clear the bar; the D-Short scores a little lower because it intentionally omits the full risk register.

> **Before you send (confirm these):**
> - [ ] Jane's committee assignments. Section 3 recommends a committee-based transparency path; confirm she sits on the relevant committee, or the move needs adjusting. Source: city clerk.
> - [ ] FY27 budget vote date. Draft says May 15; confirm against the posted council calendar.
> - [ ] 2027 reelection filing deadline. Referenced but not dated; confirm with the elections office.

> **Assumptions made:**
> 1. That the three-member group holds together on schools. If Councillor Smith is less aligned than public statements suggest, the "build to four" math in Sections 4 and 6 shifts.
> 2. That the level-services school number is achievable in FY27. The budget recommendation in Move A depends on it.

> **Warnings:**
> - The roadmap names colleagues and characterizes their likely positions. Read Section 3 before you send so you are not surprised by anything in a relationship you manage.
> - Modeled priority scores look precise (86 vs. 61). Be ready to say plainly that these are estimates from public data, not a poll.

> **Talking points:**
> - D-Short: "Here is the action version, three pages, your moves for the next six months."
> - D-Long: "The full reference. Read it when you are planning a committee agenda or prepping for the budget hearing."
> - Modeled data: "We estimated resident priorities from public data. When we run polling this summer, we replace the estimates with real numbers."

---

## What this example shows

- The **D-Short is the EO's working document** and the **AM summary is yours.** They are different audiences and should read differently.
- **Every factual claim carries an inline citation** at first use, and **modeled data is labeled every time** it appears.
- The **scoring snapshot is one line**, not the centerpiece. Accuracy and the "before you send" checklist are what protect the relationship.
