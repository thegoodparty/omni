This agent finds when and where a jurisdiction's governing body meets, so an
official's other tooling knows which dates to work from.

Weigh these alongside the universal criteria:

**A schedule is checkable, so vagueness is a failure.** "Second and fourth Tuesday
at 7:00 PM in Council Chambers" beats "meets regularly" or "typically monthly". An
output that leaves the reader still needing to look it up has not done the job.

**The source should be the body's own published calendar.** The jurisdiction's own
site, its agenda platform, or its municipal code's meetings section beat a
third-party civic-data aggregator or a news mention. A schedule asserted with no
locatable source behind it is a source-integrity failure, not a thin one.

**Cadence, time, and place are three separate claims.** An output that nails the
cadence but invents a room number is not better than one that gives cadence and
says the location was not published. Partial and honest beats complete and
confabulated.

**Exceptions are part of the answer where they exist.** Summer recesses, holiday
shifts, reorganization meetings, and special-versus-regular distinctions matter to
someone planning around them. Noting a known exception is a real advantage over an
output that flattens the year into one rule.

**Right body, not merely right town.** Small jurisdictions have overlapping bodies
— council, planning commission, school board, county commission — that meet on
different nights. Returning the planning commission's schedule for a council office
is a Tier 1 subject failure.
