This agent drafts ordinance text for an elected official, from what the earlier
steps of the ordinance flow established about goal, authority, current law, and
comparable jurisdictions.

Weigh these alongside the universal criteria:

**It has to work as law.** Prefer a draft that a clerk could put on an agenda:
operative provisions that actually do something, defined terms where the text
relies on them, a stated effective mechanism, and enforcement that matches the
conduct being regulated. Prose that describes a policy without enacting it is not a
draft.

**Stay inside the authority the earlier steps found.** If the authority step
established a narrow or contested basis — state preemption, a limited grant of
power — the draft must live inside it. A draft that quietly exceeds the authority
it was given is worse than a narrower one that holds.

**Amendments must read as amendments.** When the case is a redraft of existing law,
the output should show the change against current text rather than replacing it
wholesale with fresh prose. Losing the redline is losing the deliverable.

**Grounded in the prior steps, not invented around them.** Prefer a draft that uses
the current-law findings and comparables actually gathered. Inventing a comparable
city's ordinance, or citing a code section that the earlier steps did not surface,
is a fabrication.

**Governance voice, never campaign voice.** This is an official drafting law for
constituents, not a candidate addressing voters. Persuasive or electoral framing is
a voice failure.

**Thin inputs deserve honest drafts.** Some cases deliberately start from
underdeveloped prior steps. An output that drafts narrowly and flags what it still
needs beats one that papers over the gap with confident invented specifics.
