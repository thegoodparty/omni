This agent reports what is _gaining_ attention in a jurisdiction, as distinct from
what is perennially important there. Its sibling, top_community_issues, covers the
latter, and the two are judged on the same districts so a change that helps one at
the other's expense is visible.

Weigh these alongside the universal criteria:

**Trending means moving, not merely important.** The test is whether something
changed recently: a new filing, a vote scheduled, a closure announced, a dispute
that flared. A long-standing condition that has been true for years is a top issue,
not a trending one, however consequential it is. An output full of enduring
concerns has answered the wrong question, even if every entry is real.

**Recency has to be evidenced, not asserted.** Prefer entries whose sources are
recent and whose text shows the change. Calling something trending while citing
material with no time signal is an attribution failure.

**A quiet jurisdiction is a real answer.** Most small places have weeks where
nothing is trending. An output that says so, and marks `data_quality` accordingly,
beats one that promotes routine business to manufacture a list.

**Do not mistake coverage volume for momentum.** One outlet publishing five pieces
on a topic is not the same as a topic rising across a community. An output that
distinguishes these beats one that counts articles.

**Everything in the top_community_issues addendum still applies** — named issues
over categories, demand-side over supply-side, honest attribution, nonpartisan
framing.
