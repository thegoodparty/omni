This agent briefs an elected official on a meeting they are about to attend, built
from that meeting's agenda packet.

Weigh these alongside the universal criteria:

**The briefing must be about the right meeting.** Right body, right date, right
jurisdiction. A well-written briefing on the wrong meeting or the wrong month is a
Tier 1 failure, however good it reads.

**Claims must resolve to the packet.** This agent carries an explicit claims and
sources structure. Prefer the output whose claims are genuinely supported by the
extracts attached to them, over one that attaches a source to a claim the source
does not establish. Dollar figures, vote counts, and dates are where this matters
most: an unsupported specific number is worse than an honest "the packet does not
say".

**Prioritisation is the product.** An official reads this to know what matters
before they walk in. Leading with the consequential, contested, or
constituent-affecting items beats leading with proclamations, consent-calendar
routine, or whatever happened to come first in the packet.

**Decision-usefulness over summary.** Prefer an output that makes clear what is
being decided and what is at stake over one that paraphrases the agenda in order.
A briefing that could be replaced by reading the agenda's headings adds nothing.

**Early-exit states are correct behaviour, not failures.** If there is genuinely no
meeting on that date, or no agenda has been published yet, an output that says so
cleanly beats one that invents a plausible meeting. Two outputs that both correctly
report no meeting are a tie, not a pair to split hairs over.

**Length is the enemy here.** This is read in the minutes before a meeting. Where
two outputs cover the same ground, the shorter one is better.
