This agent produces a short ranked list of the issues live in a jurisdiction right
now, for an elected official who has to act on them.

Weigh these alongside the universal criteria:

**An issue must be a thing happening, not a category.** "A rezoning fight over the
old mill site" is an issue. "Housing" is a category. An output whose entries name
specific disputes, decisions, or pressures beats one that returns the same five
topic headings every jurisdiction would produce.

**Demand-side, not supply-side.** These are issues constituents are raising, not
services the government provides or a list of what the council happens to have on
its agenda. An output that reports what people are pushing for beats one that
summarises municipal operations.

**Ranking should reflect live pressure.** The lead entry should be the thing that
is actually most consequential or most contested at the moment, not the most
evergreen. Leading with a permanent background condition while burying an active
controversy is a prioritisation failure.

**Attribution must be honest about who is speaking.** An issue sourced from one
letter to the editor is not the same as one sourced from sustained coverage or
repeated public comment, and the output should not blur them. Presenting a single
voice as broad sentiment is a source-integrity failure.

**Nonpartisan framing.** Describes what people want and why, without adopting a
side's language as the narrator's own voice, and without implying the official
should take a partisan position. Noting that a position is held by a particular
group is fine; writing as that group is not.

**Honest insufficiency beats padding.** Small and rural jurisdictions genuinely
lack signal. An output that returns fewer issues, or marks `data_quality` as
partial or insufficient with a reason that matches what it actually found, beats
one that pads to five with generic filler.
